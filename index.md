These are my notes from the BEng Computing at Imperial College London. I've put them online in the hope that they help students coming through the course after me.

> [!Warning] Unofficial
> These are a student's notes, written while learning the material. They will contain mistakes, and they aren't endorsed by Imperial or the lecturers. Treat the lectures and official material as the source of truth.

# How these notes work
The notes are **atomic**: each one covers a single idea, like a definition, a theorem, an algorithm or a technique, and links to the ideas it builds on.

That means they aren't a textbook. Reading a module's notes from top to bottom won't flow the way the lectures do. They work best **alongside** the course:
* Met something in a lecture you didn't quite get? Search for it and read that one note.
* Stuck on a note? Follow its links back to the ideas it depends on.
* Revising? Start from a module page and jump between topics as you need them.

> [!Info] Lecturers change
> Each module page lists the lecturers who taught it when I took it (2025/26), as tags at the top. Lecturers change between years, and when they do, a module's content and emphasis can change too. If yours are different, expect these notes to differ from your course in places.

# Finding your way around
* Click **Imperial Computing Notes** at the top left to come back to this page from anywhere.
* **Search** with the bar at the top left, or press `Ctrl` + `K` (`⌘` + `K` on a Mac).
* **Hover** over any link to preview that note without leaving the page.
* The **graph** on the right shows the notes linked to the one you're reading. The icon in its corner opens the graph of every note.
* **Backlinks**, below the graph, list the notes that link to the page you're on.
* Each **module page** lists all of its topics in the order they were taught.
